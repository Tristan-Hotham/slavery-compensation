## Summary of the contribution (150 words)

The Legacies of British Slavery database traces the British addresses of people who owned enslaved people, or claimed compensation, when Britain abolished colonial slavery and paid £20 million to owners while the enslaved received nothing. This paper gives a per-capita, county-level description of the geocoded addresses for the whole of Great Britain, set against the 1831 census, with a plan written before the analysis. London holds 36% of the 3,187 addresses; Middlesex 33% against 8% of the population (county Gini 0.51). Scotland matches England per head; Wales is far lower. A planned Poisson model finds addresses thinning with distance from the four West India ports, but the gradient is carried by town addresses and depends on London and the South-East (it is not robust to leaving that region out), and listed great houses, a landed-wealth placebo, show none. Matching to the national lists, an estimated 44% of country-house addresses are listed buildings today. The paper states the database's own caveats throughout.
