## Framing and tone (fixed before analysis)

- The Slavery Abolition Act 1833 freed enslaved people in most British colonies from 1 August 1834, subject to an unpaid "apprenticeship" that lasted to 1838. Parliament voted £20 million to compensate the **slave-owners** for the loss of their "property". The enslaved people received nothing. Every page says this plainly, near the top, with sources (LBS; Draper 2010; Hall et al. 2014).
- Words: "enslaved people", not "slaves", except in quoted historical terms ("slave-owner" is the LBS term and is kept). No celebratory language, no "fortunes" puns, no superlatives about wealth.
- The data are addresses linked to people recorded by the Legacies of British Slavery (LBS) database (slave-owners, awardees, claimants and others with a recorded tie to the compensation or to slave-ownership). The address file has no compensation amounts, so this study maps **where the people lived**, not **where the money went**. The title uses "slavery money came home" as a metaphor for that; the text says so.
- LBS caveat, quoted verbatim on the page, blog and paper: "This map is a search tool and a general guide. More work is needed to produce data for statistical analysis." Results describe the geocoded subset.
- Privacy and respect: no personal names are republished in the data files, map popups or tables. Popups show town, county and the number of linked people, with a link to the LBS address page. A handful of well-known cases may be named in the text only where LBS itself or a published study discusses them (and cited), not as a lookup.

Text has HTML entities (`&amp;`) to unescape. County names vary ("Middlesed", "Kircudbrightshire", "Lancashire, Merseyside", "North Yorkshire", "Angus", "Forfarshire"...): mapped by hand to the historic (pre-1889) county in `code/county_map.json`, every mapping listed.

The pop column looks like 1841 for most counties but not all (Middlesex 132 and Surrey 182 thousand cannot be whole-county figures): I will not use it for rates, only report the comparison.
- Population: 1831 census county totals, from the Census of Great Britain 1851, Population Tables I, Vol. I, Table XXX (England and Wales) and Table X (Scotland), transcribed by Ditto Books (dittobooks.co.uk, "Census population 1801 to 1851"). Hard-coded in `code/pop1831.py` and checked against the printed totals for England (13,090,523), Wales (806,274) and Scotland (2,364,386). Ireland is excluded from per-capita work (no Irish county populations in hand).
- Listed buildings: the author's harmonised national lists (The Listed Kingdom, Hotham 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23761429; Historic England, Historic Environment Scotland, Cadw; OGL v3) from `/home/claude/newdata/raw2/listed-buildings/data/listed_*.csv.gz` (name, grade, type, point).
- Historic counties for England and Wales on the map and for assigning listed buildings to counties: the 1851 parish centroids of Caprettini and Voth (2020) replication data (openICPSR E117044, CC BY 4.0; their county field GAZ_CNTY), via `/home/claude/src6/captain-swing/data/parishes.csv`. Each point is given the county of its nearest parish centroid; the map outlines are Voronoi cells of those centroids dissolved by county and clipped to the coast (an approximation, shown as such). No historic county outlines for Scotland are available in the workspace: Scottish counties appear on the map as circles at the mean point of their geocoded addresses (or the county town if none). The Historic County Borders Project outlines would allow a full choropleth if one is wanted.

## Definitions (fixed now)

- **Analysis set**: geocoded rows with country England, Wales or Scotland (Great Britain). Ireland, the Channel Islands and overseas rows are counted and set aside. Rows with no coordinates are dropped and counted.
- **Linked people**: number of entries in the occupancies list; distinct people counted by LBS person id.
- **Geocode precision** from the OSM class: *exact* (building, amenity, leisure, tourism, historic, office, shop, man_made, railway), *street* (highway), *area* (place, boundary, landuse, natural, missing). Reported for every subset.
- **Address form** (from the street text, rules in `code/rules.py`):
  - *street address*: a house number, or a street word (Street, Square, Place, Terrace, Crescent, Row, Road, Lane, Buildings, Parade, Circus, Gardens, Walk, Court when it follows a number, Hill when it follows a number, Gate, Wharf, Quay, Yard, Inn, Chambers, Mews, Green when numbered), or OSM class highway;
  - *named house*: a named property without a number or street word (for example "X Hall", "X House", "X Park", "X Castle", "X Lodge", or a bare name such as "Olantigh");
  - *settlement only*: no street text, just a town, village, parish or district.
- **London**: LBS region "London" (the metropolis as LBS defines it, parts of Middlesex, Surrey and Kent).
- **Town or country** (exploratory, England and Wales only): the 1801 population density of the nearest 1851 parish (Caprettini and Voth's `density`, persons per km2, from the 1801 census). Urban if density is 1,000 or more, else rural. This is an imperfect, period-appropriate proxy; it is not used for Scotland.
- **Country-house candidates**: named houses in Great Britain outside London whose name contains Hall, House, Park, Court, Castle, Abbey, Manor, Grange, Priory, Place, Lodge, Towers, Mount, Hill or Wood, or is a bare single name (the rules file lists them), and that are not in an urban 1801 parish (England and Wales). 
- **Listed today**: a candidate is matched if a listed entry within 2 km (exact or street geocode) or 5 km (area geocode) has a name whose distinctive part (the name with generic words such as house, hall, park, the, and removed) matches the candidate's distinctive part (rapidfuzz token_set_ratio at least 90 and the first distinctive word shared). I will hand-check 50 random matches and 50 random non-matches by reading the name pairs and nearby listed names, and report the precision and the share of missed matches, with the corrected share.
- **Listed great houses** (for the county comparison, England and Wales): listed entries of type "house" at Grade I or II* (England, Wales) whose name contains Hall, House, Park, Court, Castle, Abbey, Manor, Grange, Priory or Place and not farm, farmhouse, cottage, barn, lodge, stable, gate, wall, almshouse, school, vicarage or rectory. Assigned to historic counties by nearest 1851 parish.

## Questions and pre-specified tests

Unit: the historic county (Great Britain: 41 English counties with Yorkshire as one, 12 Welsh, 33 Scottish), with 1831 population. Outcome: geocoded LBS addresses (main), linked people (alternative).

- **Q1 Concentration (descriptive with intervals).** Share of GB addresses in London and in Middlesex against their share of GB population; Gini coefficient of addresses across counties relative to population (Lorenz curve of counties ordered by rate), with a bootstrap 95% interval (resampling addresses). Top 15 towns (by town_city, merged spellings) with their counts and the share of the GB total in the top 5. Expectation: London holds more than a quarter of GB addresses against about a tenth of GB population; Gini above 0.5. Against: London share under twice its population share.
- **H2 Atlantic ports.** Counties with a major West India port in the 1830s (London: Middlesex; Bristol: Gloucestershire; Liverpool: Lancashire; Glasgow and Greenock: Lanarkshire and Renfrewshire) have higher rates per 10,000 people in 1831, and rates fall with distance from the nearest of London, Bristol, Liverpool and Glasgow. Model (main): Poisson GLM of county address counts with offset log(1831 population), regressors: port county (0/1), log distance (km) from the county's population-weighted centre (mean of its 1851 parish centroids weighted by 1801 parish population for England and Wales; county town for Scotland) to the nearest of the four port cities, and nation (Wales, Scotland vs England); robust (HC1) standard errors; report incidence rate ratios with 95% CIs and N. Against H2: IRR for distance at or above 1 or CI including 1.
  - Alternatives and checks: (1) negative binomial; (2) linked people instead of addresses; (3) all LBS addresses including the unlocated ones (LBS county table), which tests whether geocoding gaps drive the result; (4) Middlesex dropped; (5) leave one region out (LBS regions); (6) England only; (7) Scotland only (descriptive, small N); (8) placebo outcome: listed great houses per county (landed wealth proxy, England and Wales): if the LBS pattern only tracked old landed wealth, the same port and distance terms would appear for listed great houses. Expectation: they do not, or much more weakly.
- **Q3 Town and country (descriptive).** Shares of street addresses, named houses and settlement-only records, in London, the rest of England, Wales and Scotland, with geocode precision. Exploratory: the 1801-density urban share for England and Wales outside London.
- **Q4 Country houses and listing (descriptive with a correction).** Share of country-house candidates that match a listed building today, overall and by nation, corrected by the hand check. By county (England and Wales): LBS country-house candidates per 100 listed great houses, and its Spearman correlation with the county address rate (H4: positive; against: rho at or below 0).
- **Q5 Dates (descriptive).** Where occupancy start and end years are given, their distribution, to show what period the addresses describe (expected mostly 1800s to 1850s).

Caveats to state: LBS's own warning; geocoded subset only (3,115 of 5,138 addresses in the LBS county table were located, with coverage varying by county); addresses are where people lived at some point, not necessarily in 1834; some people have several addresses, some addresses several people; compensation amounts are not in this file; area geocodes (towns, parishes) place an address at a centroid; the listed match is approximate; associations only, no causal claims.
